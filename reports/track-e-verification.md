## What would mislead a learner

- Claim 3 says there are six lawful bases. Since 5 February 2026 Article 6(1) lists seven. Point (ea) is recognised legitimate interest.
- Claim 3 cites Article 5(1)(c) for purpose limitation. Purpose limitation is Article 5(1)(b).
- Claim 8 presents paragraph 8 as general equality monitoring. It covers four categories only. A learner would wrongly think it covers trade union membership, sex life, genetic or biometric data.
- Claim 8 asks about a new substantial public interest condition. The revised Schedule 1 shows none. The 2025 Act widened paragraphs 10 and 14 instead.
- Claim 15 says the Act replaced an "essentially equivalent" standard. That phrase was never in the UK statute. It came from EU case law.
- Claim 16 suggests the EEA has its own adequacy regulations. The EEA is covered by a transitional provision in DPA 2018 Schedule 21.
- Claims 9 and 20 name the ICO. Since 30 September 2026 the UK GDPR text refers to "the Commission" (the Information Commission). Lessons that tell you to consult the ICO need the new name and a date.
- Claim 6 says recognised legitimate interests are "not for commercial processing". The Act does not say that. Teach the closed Annex 1 list instead.
- Claims 10 to 13 rest on the DMBOK2 book. Mark them as checked against the book or flag them `unverified: true` until someone checks the printed text.
- The ICO and DMBOK2 claims (4, 5, 10 to 14, 20) and the foreign law claims (23 to 26) need a second pass from an environment that reaches ico.org.uk, dama.org, EUR-Lex and the national sites.
